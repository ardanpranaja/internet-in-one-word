# Candidate Words — The Internet in One Word

Competition: u-ronigrv-the-internet-in-one-word-e388f33b
Pot: $10 (1st $5, 2nd $3, 3rd $2). Deadline: 2026-11-26 17:15 UTC.
Brief: one word for the whole internet, funniest and most fitting wins.
Gate: `/output/entry.txt` must be UTF-8 text of 1 to 30 characters, not only
white space. Judge (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, comedy-editor persona) compares two
words at a time and picks the funnier, better-fitting one, weighing in order:

1. A single word that describes the whole internet (a phrase, sentence or
   list loses to any single word that captures the internet).
2. Earns a laugh: the word is funny by itself, with no explanation needed.
3. Says something true about the internet as a whole, rather than one app,
   one site or one mood.
4. Surprises: it is not the first obvious word anyone would reach for.
5. Lands in one beat: spelled correctly, instantly readable, short enough
   to hit before the reader thinks.

Plus: the best word makes a reader laugh and then nod; shock for its own
sake does not count. Tie-breaks favour the smaller text (fewer bytes), then
the earlier submission — so short is doubly good.

Rules I applied: one word only, no hyphens-as-phrases, no proper nouns of a
single site/app, no shock/crude words, no invented words the judge would
have to decode, original pick throughout.

## Scoring (against the five criteria, best first)

- **slop** — C1: one word, names the substance of the internet itself.
  C2: funny on its own — "slop" is a viscerally funny word, and calling
  the *entire* internet slop lands with no explanation. C3: true about the
  whole internet, not one app — the flood of low-effort AI content is
  everywhere now. C4: surprising — not the first word most people reach
  for as a description of the whole internet. C5: 4 characters, one beat,
  instantly readable, and the smallest text of any serious contender (the
  tie-break favours it). The laugh-then-nod test: laugh at "slop", nod
  because it is true.
- **brainrot** — C1/C3 strong: it names what the internet does to you,
  and it is true of the whole feed, not one site. C2 funny, C5 short and
  readable. Weaker than slop on C4 (it was word of the year in 2024, so
  the judge has heard it a lot) and on C2: it is more of a knowing nod
  than a laugh. Also 8 bytes to slop's 4.
- **feral** — C2 genuinely funny applied to the internet, C4 surprising,
  C5 5 characters. Weaker on C3: "feral" is a vibe that could describe
  anything wild; it says less that is specifically true about the
  internet than slop does, and it is an adjective, not the substance.
- **unhinged** — funny and true, but C4 is its problem: "unhinged" is one
  of the most common adjectives already attached to the internet, so it
  is close to the first obvious word. Loses to slop on surprise.
- **hellsite** — funny, short, surprising. C3 is its problem: the word
  was coined for one site (Twitter/X), so it describes one corner of the
  internet rather than the whole of it, which criterion 3 penalises.
- **enshittification** — the funniest word in the list to people who know
  it, and true about platform decay. Fails C5 outright: 17 characters,
  hard to spell, not instantly readable to everyone; the brief explicitly
  wants a word that hits before the reader thinks.
- **doomscroll / doomscrolling** — true and widely understood, but C3
  describes one behaviour (scrolling) rather than the whole internet, and
  C4 fails: it is the first obvious word half the field will reach for.
- **clickbait, memes, trolls, notifications** — each names one corner of
  the internet (ads, jokes, commenters, the phone buzzing), so all fail
  C3, the "as a whole" test, even where they are funny.
- **procrastination, distraction, infinite, void, echochamber** — true,
  but not funny by themselves (C2 fails): they read as descriptions, not
  jokes. "Void" is the best of them (the "scream into the void" echo) but
  it is poetic rather than funny.
- **yapping, cooked, mid, loud** — slang-funny and short, but each
  describes one mood or one judgement, not the internet as a whole (C3),
  and "mid"/"loud" are too thin to beat a word with a real image behind
  it.
- **dumpsterfire** — funny and vivid, but a well-worn cliché phrase by
  now, so it scores low on C4, and at 12 characters it is three times the
  size of slop.

## Head-to-heads that decided it

- slop vs brainrot: both single words, both true. "Slop" is funnier
  standing alone (the pig-trough image), more surprising as a name for
  the whole internet, and half the size — it wins on C2, C4 and C5.
- slop vs feral: "feral" is a funny adjective; "slop" is a funnier noun
  that names what the internet *is* now, not just how it behaves.
- slop vs unhinged: "unhinged" is expected; "slop" surprises, and the
  surprise is earned — the laugh comes with a nod, not a wince.

## Chosen entry

> slop

4 characters, one word, UTF-8, no white space. Submitted as entry.txt.
